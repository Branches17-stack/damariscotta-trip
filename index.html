Damariscotta Lake

About 4,400 acres, 10 miles long, 21 miles of shoreline. Mean depth 30 ft, max 114 ft. Satellite view shows the weed beds and shoals.

House pin is approximate. Drag it to the right cove.

What's in it

x1Smallmouth bassEverywhere. Best on the middle-basin islands and rocky points. Fish to 5 lb.
x1Largemouth bassSouth basin weed edges and docks.
x1White perchWeedy shorelines. Small jigs, worms.
x1Chain pickerelWeed lines, south basin. Spinners and streamers.
x2Landlocked salmonNorth basin, stocked. Troll streamers.
x2Brown troutNorth basin, stocked since 1993.
x2Lake trout (togue)North basin, stocked since 1972. Deep hole off the state park.

Late September

Surface 69 to 71°F and cooling. The thermocline is breaking up and salmonids are moving shallower, but for one day, bass are still the highest-percentage target.

  • Smallmouth: lead-head jigs with yellow sparkle-tail or curly-tail grubs (the local standard), tubes, Ned rigs, drop shot, small crankbaits. 8 to 20 ft on structure.
  • Largemouth: Texas-rigged worms on weed edges, spinnerbaits, topwater at first light.
  • Pickerel and perch: inline spinners, Mickey Finn streamers, small jigs.
  • Salmon and togue: troll streamers or smelt-pattern spoons in the north basin; go deep near the park.

Licenses and limits

  • Non-resident, online at maine.gov/ifw: 1-day $18, 3-day $30, 7-day $62, plus agent fee. Buy before Friday.
  • General law: bass 2 per day, only 1 over 14 in. Salmon 2 per day, 14 in minimum. Togue 2 per day, 18 in minimum. Lakes open through September 30. Check the Damariscotta Lake listing for special rules.
  • Catch, photo, release for the tournament and none of this applies.

Friday tournament

7:30 AM to 2:30 PM. Catch, photo, release. Log each fish here and the math is done for you.

Scoring

  • 1 point per legal fish, 8 in minimum, plus 1 point per inch, to the nearest quarter inch.
  • Multiply by species: bass, perch, pickerel x1; salmon, brown, togue x2.
  • First fish of the day +5. Three or more species +10.
  • Ties: biggest bass, then first fish.
  • Low score buys the Oxbow round and the Banyan Tree order. Winner holds the trophy. Biggest bass picks the Saturday hike.
  • Every fish photographed on the board next to the token lure. No photo, no points.

Packing

Each person

  • Base layer, fleece or light puffy, one more warm layer for the boat.
  • Waterproof shell for Friday morning. Rain pants optional.
  • Hiking shoes or trail runners, wool socks.
  • Quick-dry pants, shorts, warm hat, ball cap, polarized sunglasses, light gloves.
  • For the crossing: windproof outer layer, non-marking shoes, dry socks in a dry bag. It runs 10 to 15 degrees colder on the water.
  • Swimsuit, camp shoes, dry bag for phone and wallet.

Fishing

  • One medium spinning combo each, 6'6" to 7', 8 to 12 lb line or braid with a fluoro leader. One heavier rod if anyone trolls.
  • Jig heads 1/8 to 3/8 oz, yellow sparkle-tail and curly-tail grubs, tubes, Ned rigs, drop-shot hooks and weights, Texas-rig worms, two small crankbaits, inline spinners, Mickey Finn streamers, a couple of smelt-pattern spoons.
  • Pliers, cutters, net, measuring board, towel, small tackle tray.
  • License bought online before Friday.

Buy in Bangor or Waldoboro

  • Ice, beer, coolers, coffee, breakfast and lunch food, trash bags, paper towels, sunscreen, bug spray, firewood if the house has none, battery bank or hotspot.
